# Lab 5: Can You Trust This AI Brief? — The RupeeRise Case

## Case

RupeeRise is a fictional digital lending app in Bengaluru that gives small personal loans to young salaried workers. Its founder is meeting investors next week and asked an AI tool to write a one-page brief on India's digital lending and UPI market.

The founder asks: **"The brief reads really well. Can I send it to investors as it is?"**

As the Business Analyst, I must check every factual claim before the brief is shared.

## Accepted proof sources

| Claim category | Official source |
|---|---|
| UPI and payments data | [NPCI](https://www.npci.org.in/product/upi/product-statistics) |
| Lending rules and circulars | [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/) |
| Government announcements | [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/) |
| Company figures | Company's annual report or official press release |
| Not accepted as proof | Blogs, social media, unsourced news, or another AI answer |

## Why this matters

AI can produce fluent and confident text while including wrong, outdated, distorted or invented information. This is called a hallucination. A link existing does not prove that it supports the claim. Every number, rule, quotation and citation must be opened and checked.

**Important:** RupeeRise is fictional. No company performance, customer count, lending volume or financial result is assumed for it.
